\begin{abstract}
Triathlon combines swimming, cycling and running, and its triple nature generates
three interconnected decision problems: how to allocate training load, which races
to enter, and how to pace a race. This project models all three with Linear
Programming (LP) and 0--1 Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP), using thirty-two months of
the author's real training data (574 activities from a Strava export). The models
form a pipeline: the fitness state produced by the training model gates race
selection and sets the energy budget for pacing. Beyond the base solutions, the
project exercises the full course syllabus --- Simplex-based LP solving, duality and
shadow-price interpretation, right-hand-side and objective-coefficient ranging,
an explicit primal--dual pair, Branch \& Bound with LP
relaxation gaps, and a network-flow reduction with an empirical total-unimodularity
demonstration. Several optimal solutions reproduce established coaching wisdom
(build-then-taper periodization, ``steady bike, hard run'' pacing) without it being
imposed, while sensitivity analysis reveals which recommendations are driven by the
objective and which by the constraints. A final integration step shows that the
three models are coherent only when the race-day energy budget depends on form
rather than fitness alone --- which makes the training objective derived rather
than assumed --- and resolves the circular dependency between training plan and
race calendar by iterating to a fixed point.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}

A competitive age-group triathlete faces three planning problems at different time
scales. Over a macrocycle of 12--20 weeks they must decide how many hours to train
in each sport and at which intensity, trading fitness gains against accumulated
fatigue. Over a season they must choose a race calendar under a money budget,
limited days off work, and physiological recovery requirements. Finally, on race
day they must distribute a limited energy reserve across three consecutive legs to
minimize finishing time.

These problems are usually solved by rules of thumb. This project instead treats
them as mathematical programs and asks what an optimizer would do: Model~1 is an
LP that allocates weekly training load; Model~2 is a 0--1 MIP that selects the race
calendar; Model~3 is an LP that computes a pacing strategy. The three are
coupled through the athlete's fitness state: Model~1's terminal CTL/ATL gates
race readiness in Model~2 and sets the energy budget in Model~3
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The coupling is genuinely circular --- the chosen
A-race determines the macrocycle Model~1 should plan --- and
Section~\ref{sec:coherence} resolves it by iterating to a fixed point rather
than by fixing an order, and shows what the three objectives must satisfy to
be mutually consistent.

Two features distinguish the project from a textbook exercise. First, all inputs of

course emphasizes: duals and their physiological meaning, value functions under
RHS variation, integrality gaps, and alternative optima.

\section{Literature}

The training model rests on the fitness--fatigue framework of Banister et
al.~\cite{banister1975}, applied to triathletes by Millet et al.~\cite{millet2002}
and criticised by Hellard et al.~\cite{hellard2006}; taper practice follows
Mujika~\cite{mujika2009}, with a recent optimization treatment in Ceddia et
al.~\cite{ceddia2025}. Race selection is a knapsack-type selection problem in
the integer-programming modelling tradition~\cite{nemhauser1988,williams2013};
sports scheduling as an IP application area is surveyed by
Ribeiro~\cite{ribeiro2012} and advocated pedagogically by Trick~\cite{trick2005}.
Pacing is reviewed by Abbiss and Laursen~\cite{abbiss2008}; the cycling--running
asymmetries behind our coupling constraint are documented by Millet et
al.~\cite{millet2009} and triathlon-specific factors by Wu et al.~\cite{wu2014},
with a numerical-optimization approach in Ni et al.~\cite{ni2022}. General LP/OR
background follows Dantzig~\cite{dantzig1963} and
Hillier--Lieberman~\cite{hillier2015}.

\section{Data}

The data pipeline processes the author's Strava bulk export: 598 activities from
October 2023 to June 2026, of which 574 swim/bike/run sessions are kept. Each
activity contributes date, sport, moving time, average heart rate (HR) and, for
cycling, average power. For every activity a Banister training impulse is computed,
\[
\text{TRIMP} \;=\; t \,\cdot\, \text{HRr} \cdot 0.64\, e^{1.92\,\text{HRr}},
\qquad
\text{HRr} = \frac{\text{HR} - \text{HR}_{\text{rest}}}{\text{HR}_{\max} - \text{HR}_{\text{rest}}},
\]
with $t$ in minutes. The 125 activities without HR (mostly older runs) receive a
sport-specific default intensity. Daily loads are smoothed exponentially into a
chronic training load (CTL, $\tau = 42$ days) and an acute training load (ATL,
$\tau = 7$ days); the balance $\text{TSB} = \text{CTL} - \text{ATL}$ measures
freshness. At the time of the study the athlete's state was
$\text{CTL}_0 = 78.1$, $\text{ATL}_0 = 91.5$, $\text{TSB} = -13.4$: solid fitness,
currently fatigued.

Each activity is also classified into an intensity bucket --- easy (Z1--2), moderate
(Z3), hard (Z4+) --- relative to a \emph{sport-specific} lactate-threshold HR (run
173, bike 166, swim 164 bpm), since HR reads systematically lower in cycling and
swimming at equal effort. The TRIMP-per-hour rates $r_{db}$ by (sport, bucket) that
parameterize Model~1 are estimated from the data where at least five activities
exist and from the Banister formula at the bucket's representative HR otherwise
(4 of 9 cells, flagged as theoretical).

Candidate races for Model~2 are a constructed but documented dataset of 17 events
(Greek domestic, European, and long-haul; entry fees at official price ranges,
travel costs and vacation-day requirements by destination), with deliberate
structural features: same-weekend conflicts, a championship pair two weeks apart,
and a high-value early-season race the athlete cannot be fit for. The anchor

Kraichgau and Nice, WTCS Hamburg, London T100, Chicago Triathlon, Epidavros
Legacy, the Hellenic Championship on the federation's published calendar);
slots without a verifiable counterpart are explicitly labeled representative.
All assumptions and per-race verification statuses are recorded in the
repository (\texttt{docs/race\_data\_assumptions.md}).

\section{Solution Method}
\label{sec:solution}

All three models are written in Python with PuLP as the algebraic modelling
layer and solved with CBC, the toolchain used in the course. Each model is
built by a \texttt{build\_model} function that returns the
\texttt{pulp.LpProblem} together with its variable dictionaries; a
\texttt{solve} wrapper calls \texttt{PULP\_CBC\_CMD} and returns the status
string, and an \texttt{extract\_solution} function converts the solved problem
into data frames of the plan, the trajectory and the dual values. Keeping these
three steps separate is what allows every sensitivity experiment in
Sections~\ref{sec:m1duality} and~\ref{sec:sensitivity} to rebuild and re-solve
the same model under perturbed data without duplicating any formulation.

Models~1 and~3 are pure linear programs and CBC solves them with the simplex
method; Appendix~\ref{sec:simplex} works a two-variable reduction of Model~3 by
hand, through Phase~I and a single Phase~II pivot, to make the algorithm
explicit rather than implicit. Model~2 is a 0--1 program and is solved by
branch and bound on the LP relaxation; the relaxation is also solved on its own
so that the integrality gap and the fractional variables can be inspected, and
clique cuts are added separately to show how a tighter formulation closes part
of that gap. Because every logical condition in Model~2 is expressed through
standard linear templates rather than big-$M$ terms, the relaxation is tight
enough that branch and bound terminates immediately at this size.

\section{Model 1 --- Optimal Training-Load Allocation (LP)}

\subsection{Formulation}

For disciplines $D=\{\text{swim},\text{bike},\text{run}\}$, buckets
$B=\{\text{easy},\text{mod},\text{hard}\}$ and weeks $W=\{1,\dots,T\}$ ($T=16$ for
an Olympic-distance goal), the decision variables are training hours
$h_{dbw}\ge 0$ ($3\times3\times16 = 144$ variables). Weekly load and the Banister
recursion enter as equality-defined auxiliaries, keeping the model an LP:
\begin{align*}
L_w &= \sum_{d,b} r_{db}\, h_{dbw},\\
\text{CTL}_w &= \lambda_c\, \text{CTL}_{w-1} + (1-\lambda_c)\,\tfrac{L_w}{7},
&\lambda_c &= e^{-7/42},\\
\text{ATL}_w &= \lambda_a\, \text{ATL}_{w-1} + (1-\lambda_a)\,\tfrac{L_w}{7},
&\lambda_a &= e^{-7/7}.
\end{align*}
The objective maximizes predicted race-day performance in the Banister sense,
\[
\max\; p_T = k_1\,\text{CTL}_T - k_2\,\text{ATL}_T, \qquad (k_1,k_2)=(1,2).
\]
Banister units are arbitrary: the \emph{level} of $p_T$ (here negative, since
$k_2 > k_1$) has no physical meaning --- only differences between plans and the
marginal values (duals) are interpretable.
Maximizing TSB alone would be degenerate (the optimum would be to rest);
the Banister form rewards early CTL accumulation, whose contribution persists,
while early-week ATL decays away by race day. The constraints, for every week $w$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{d,b} h_{dbw} &\le H^{\max}_w
    && \text{phase-dependent hours ceiling (12\,h baseline)}\\
h^{\min}_d \le \sum_{b} h_{dbw} &\le h^{\max}_d \quad \forall d
    && \text{discipline bounds (pool access, injury risk)}\\
\sum_{d} \left(h_{d,\text{mod},w} + h_{d,\text{hard},w}\right)
    &\le 0.2 \sum_{d,b} h_{dbw}
    && \text{80/20 polarized-training rule}\\
\text{ATL}_w &\le 110
    && \text{overtraining guard}\\
L_w &\le 1.1\, L_{w-1}
    && \text{ramp-rate limit } (w \ge 2)\\
L_w &\le \theta_w\, \bar{L}_{\text{build}} \quad w \in \text{taper}
    && \theta_{15} = 0.6,\; \theta_{16} = 0.4
\end{align}
where $\bar{L}_{\text{build}}$ is the (linear) average build-phase load. The solved
LP has 192 variables and 209 constraints.

\subsection{Duality and sensitivity}
\label{sec:m1duality}

\paragraph{RHS ranging.} Re-solving for hour ceilings $H^{\max}\in[8,16]$ traces
the LP value function (Fig.~\ref{fig:m1rhs}): empirically piecewise-linear and
concave, with slopes $1.55 \to 0.94 \to 0.12 \to 0.06 \to 0$. Below
$8.5$\,h the model is infeasible (discipline minimums collide with taper caps);
above $10.5$\,h extra hours are worthless because the ATL ceiling binds first. For
this athlete, recovery capacity --- not free time --- limits performance.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.52\textwidth]{model1_rhs_ranging.png}
\caption{Value function of the weekly-hours RHS: piecewise-linear, concave,
saturating at 10.5\,h/week.}
\label{fig:m1rhs}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Shadow prices.} The largest duals in absolute value belong to the
race-week discipline minimums: a forced hour of running in week 16 costs $12.9$
performance points --- the model would rather rest completely. The ATL-ceiling duals
peak in late build (week 12: $+0.07$ per TRIMP/day of extra fatigue tolerance),
quantifying the value of recovery capacity when it matters most.

\paragraph{Alternative optima.} Fixing the objective at $p^\ast$ and re-solving
with total season hours as a secondary objective yields plans from 124.2\,h to
162.3\,h at \emph{identical} performance: the optimal face has positive dimension,
and the same race-day form is achievable with 38 fewer training hours. Practically,
the efficient (minimum-hours) plan is preferable; theoretically, this is LP
degeneracy made visible. Realistic per-discipline weekly caps were added to bound
this degeneracy for the reported plan.

\paragraph{Benchmark against a coaching heuristic.} A standard
``fill the available hours, 20/45/35 discipline split, 80/15/5 intensity split''
plan violates the ATL ceiling in 13 of 16 weeks and scores $p_T=-69.5$ versus the
optimum $-11.4$. The heuristic does, however, arrive at race day with
$\text{TSB}=+21$, inside the band $+15\ldots+25$ recommended by coaching practice,
while the LP over-tapers to $+35$ --- see the $k_2$ discussion in
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

\subsection{Validation against actual training}

Parameter estimation from history is not validation, so the optimal plan was
compared against what the athlete \emph{actually did} in the final 16 weeks of
the export: 6.6\,h/week at a 37\% moderate+hard share, versus the optimum's
9.0\,h/week at 15\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:actual}). The athlete trains less and
substantially \emph{harder} than optimal --- the exact anti-polarized pattern the
endurance literature warns against --- and the model quantifies the cost: the
actual pattern cannot sustain the CTL trajectory the optimal plan achieves
within the same fatigue ceiling. This turns the LP from an abstract exercise
into a personally actionable diagnosis.

Duality theory itself was also verified numerically on the solved models:
the primal optimum equals the dual objective $\sum_i y_i b_i$ (strong duality)
and $y_i \cdot \text{slack}_i = 0$ holds for every constraint (complementary
slackness), on both Model~1 and Model~3, to $10^{-6}$.

\section{Model 2 --- Race-Calendar Selection (0--1 MIP)}

\subsection{Formulation}

For candidate races $i \in R$ with week $t_i$ and distance class $c_i$, binary
variables $x_i$ select the season calendar. The risk-adjusted value of a race is
\[
v_i = q_i\left(\alpha\,\text{pts}_i + \beta\,\text{strat}_i
      + \gamma\,\text{enj}_i + \varphi\,\text{fit}_i\right),
\]
where $q_i$ is the probability of good race-day weather, enjoyment
$\text{enj}_i$ aggregates subjective organization/scenery/swim scores, and
suitability $\text{fit}_i$ is computed from \emph{objective} course data ---
elevation gain per km per leg matched to the athlete's flat-course preference. The
program is a two-dimensional knapsack with temporal side constraints:
\[
\max \sum_i v_i x_i
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_i \kappa_i x_i \le 2500 \text{ EUR}, \qquad
\sum_i \delta_i x_i \le 12 \text{ days},
\]
at most two races per month; recovery spacing
$x_i + x_j \le 1$ for pairs closer than the recovery requirement of the earlier
race (sprint 1, Olympic 2, 70.3 4 weeks); at least one championship-quality
``A-race''; and a readiness gate from Model~1 --- a projected season CTL curve
(off-season start, load ramping to Model~1's build level) must reach a
class-specific requirement by race week, which excludes the high-value but
too-early Dubai 70.3.

Each side constraint instantiates a standard integer-modeling template. The
pairwise recovery rows $x_i + x_j \le 1$ are the ``at most one of two''
(either--or) pattern; the clique inequalities added below generalize it to
``at most one of $k$''; the A-race row $\sum_{i \in A} x_i \ge 1$ is the
``at least one of $N$'' (set-covering) pattern; the monthly caps are
``at most $M$ of $N$''; and the readiness gate is variable fixing,
$x_i = 0$, derived from Model~1 rather than assumed. Keeping every logical
condition in one of these linear forms is what allows the model to remain a
pure 0--1 program solvable by Branch \& Bound, with no big-$M$ terms and hence
no weak-relaxation penalty.

\subsection{Results}

\label{sec:m2results}
The optimum selects 8 of 17 races for a value of 571.7
(Table~\ref{tab:calendar}, Fig.~\ref{fig:m2cal}), spending 2250 of 2500 EUR
and 11 of 12 vacation days --- both knapsack dimensions are nearly binding. The
recovery constraint forces a choice between the National Championship and the
home-soil 70.3 two weeks later; the optimizer picks the 70.3. The LP relaxation
scores 600.5, an integrality gap of $5.0\%$, with eight fractional variables
including the championship conflict split exactly $0.5/0.5$ --- a textbook
illustration of why Branch \& Bound is needed. At the relaxation's optimum the
budget dual is $0.094$ value/EUR while the vacation-days dual is
$\approx 0$: at the baseline, money, not time off, is the scarce resource.

\paragraph{Budget sweep.} Varying the money budget from 500 to 4000 EUR
(Fig.~\ref{fig:m2budget}) shows the characteristic IP behavior: the optimal value
increases in \emph{steps} (races are indivisible) beneath the concave LP-relaxation
curve. Above 3000 EUR the value saturates at 592.7 because all 12 vacation days are
consumed --- the binding constraint switches dimension.

\paragraph{Strengthening the formulation: clique cuts.} The recovery conflicts
are encoded pairwise ($x_i + x_j \le 1$), which is integer-equivalent but weak
in relaxation: three mutually conflicting races at $x = \tfrac12$ satisfy all
pairwise constraints. Enumerating the maximal cliques of the conflict graph
(Bron--Kerbosch; 17 nodes) yields four valid inequalities
$\sum_{i \in C} x_i \le 1$ with $|C| \ge 3$ (largest clique: four August--September
races). Adding them tightens the LP relaxation from 600.5 to 591.0, closing a
third of the integrality gap ($5.04\% \to 3.38\%$) without touching the integer
optimum --- a small demonstration of the cutting-planes idea underlying modern
branch-and-cut solvers.

\subsection{The dual problem and its interpretation}

The LP relaxation of Model~2 is already in the standard primal form
$\max\{c^{T}x \mid Ax \le b,\; x \ge 0\}$, so its dual follows immediately as
$\min\{b^{T}y \mid A^{T}y \ge c,\; y \ge 0\}$. Writing it out with one dual
variable per constraint family --- $y_\mathcal{B}$ (money budget), $y_\mathcal{D}$
(vacation days), $y_m$ (races in month $m$), $y_{ij}$ (recovery conflict between
races $i$ and $j$), $y_A$ (A-race requirement) --- gives
\[
\min\; \mathcal{B}\,y_\mathcal{B} + \mathcal{D}\,y_\mathcal{D}
      + \sum_m M\,y_m + \sum_{(i,j)} y_{ij} - y_A
\]
subject to, for every candidate race $i$,
\[
\underbrace{\kappa_i\, y_\mathcal{B} + \delta_i\, y_\mathcal{D}
 + y_{\text{month}(i)} + \sum_{j:\,(i,j)\in C} y_{ij}
 - [\,i \in A\,]\, y_A}_{\text{shadow cost of the resources race } i \text{ consumes}}
\;\ge\; v_i ,
\qquad y \ge 0 .
\]
The dual constraint is the economic statement of the problem: a race enters the
optimal calendar only when the value it delivers covers the shadow cost of
everything it consumes --- euros, days off, its slot in the month, and the
races it blocks through recovery. By complementary slackness, races selected
strictly inside the budget satisfy this with equality, while rejected races
have strict surplus, and that surplus is exactly the amount by which their
value would have to rise to make them worth entering
(Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

Solving the relaxation gives 12 nonzero duals out of 27 constraints, and their
ranking is the project's most counter-intuitive result
(Table~\ref{tab:m2duals}). The money budget prices at only $0.094$ per euro and
the vacation-day budget at $\approx 0$, while the \emph{recovery} conflicts
dominate: the constraint separating the Hellenic Championship from the Costa
Navarino 70.3 two weeks later carries a shadow price of $56.4$, by far the
largest in the model. For this athlete the binding resource is neither money
nor time off work but \emph{calendar space}: the physiological requirement to
recover between races is what truly constrains the season.

\subsection{Network-flow extension}

Dropping the two knapsacks, the monthly cap and the A-race constraint leaves only
recovery spacing, and the problem becomes a \emph{longest path in a DAG}: nodes are
race-ready races in week order, an arc $i \to j$ exists when the gap respects the
recovery of race $i$, and node weights are the $v_i$. Recovery requirements
compose along chains, so feasible calendars and $S$--$T$ paths coincide. Three
methods were implemented and agree exactly ($z = 639.4$): dynamic programming in
topological order; a flow LP with \emph{continuous} variables; and the restricted
MIP. The flow LP returns an integral solution without any integrality constraints
--- its constraint matrix is a network matrix, hence totally unimodular. The
knapsack constraints are therefore precisely what breaks the network structure and
forces Branch \& Bound in the full model.

The flow formulation is also what makes the \emph{Network Simplex Method}
applicable. Its constraint matrix is the node--arc incidence matrix of the DAG
(one $+1$ and one $-1$ per arc column), so every basis corresponds to a
spanning tree of the network and a pivot is an arc exchange --- entering arc in,
leaving arc out --- performed by updating tree potentials rather than by
factorizing a general basis matrix. Because the basis is always integral, the
method terminates at an integral flow without any branching, which is the
algorithmic counterpart of the total unimodularity verified above. The present
instance (18 nodes --- 16 race-ready races plus source and sink --- and 136
arcs) is far too small for the speed advantage to
matter, so it is solved with CBC's general simplex; the point is structural,
namely that the relaxed race-selection problem belongs to the class of problems
Network Simplex is designed for, and leaves that class exactly when the two
knapsack rows are added.

\section{Model 3 --- Race Pacing Strategy (LP)}

\subsection{Formulation}

For legs $\Lambda$ and intensity zones $Z1$--$Z5$, the variables $t_{\ell z}\ge 0$
are minutes in zone $z$ during leg $\ell$. Within a zone, speed and metabolic rate
are constants, linearizing the nonlinear speed--energy curve: bike speed scales
with the cube root of power (aerodynamic drag), $v_z = \phi(\gamma)\,
v_{\text{FTP}}(P_z/\text{FTP})^{1/3}$ with $\text{FTP}=260$\,W. The factor
$\phi(\gamma) \le 1$ accounts for the course's climbing $\gamma$ (metres per
km): at equal power a rider covers less ground on a hilly course, because the
time lost climbing exceeds the time regained descending. It is obtained from
the cycling power balance $P = v\,(k v^2 + F_{\text{roll}} + m g s)$ by solving
for the up- and downhill speeds at constant power and taking their harmonic
mean over equal distances, with $k$ back-solved from the athlete's own flat
reference speed --- so nothing in $\phi$ is fitted to race results. It is convex
in $\gamma$ ($\phi = 0.97$ at 9 m/km, $0.91$ at 15), so a linear penalty would
be only a local approximation. The baseline instance is a flat reference
course, $\gamma = 0$, $\phi = 1$. Swim/run speeds are fractions of
threshold pace; energy cost per km rises with intensity (economy factors
0.95--1.20). The program minimizes total time subject to covering each leg's
distance, an overall energy budget $E^{\text{tot}} = k_E\cdot\text{CTL}_T$ linked
to Model~1, per-leg caps on time at Z4--Z5, and a bike$\to$run coupling: each kJ of
hard bike work reduces effective run coverage by $\varphi$ km.

\paragraph{The 70.3 and the fueling extension.} The athlete's
\emph{Ironman 70.3 Greece, Costa Navarino} (26 October 2025;
1.9\,km / 90\,km / 21.1\,km, finish 5:39:05, race-day $\text{CTL} = 71.4$,
$\text{TSB} = +5.6$ after a genuine taper) --- incidentally the same event, in
its 2027 edition, that Model~2 selects as the season's A-race (R14) --- exposed
a structural omission: at 70.3 distance the model is \emph{infeasible} even with
the calibrated $k_E = 142$ --- stored energy alone cannot cover a 5--6 hour
race, and indeed no athlete attempts one unfed. In-race carbohydrate intake at
$r$ kJ/min enters the model linearly, as a reduction of every zone's
\emph{net} drain:
\[
\sum_{\ell,z} (e_{\ell z} - r)\, t_{\ell z} \le E^{\text{tot}},
\]
so the extension costs nothing in tractability. Calibrating $r$ against the
actual race gives $r \approx 21$ kJ/min --- which is $\approx 75$\,g of
carbohydrate per hour, \emph{precisely the standard sports-nutrition
guideline}. With it, the model reproduces the swim within 0.4 min (33.3 vs.\
32.9) and the bike within three minutes (177.5 vs.\ 180.3).

Fueling can further be made a \emph{decision}: per-leg intake $F_\ell \ge 0$
(kJ) bounded by gut absorption, $F_\ell \le \bar r_\ell \sum_z t_{\ell z}$
(swim 0, bike 25, run 15 kJ/min), with budget
$\sum e_{\ell z} t_{\ell z} - \sum_\ell F_\ell \le E^{\text{tot}}$ --- still an
LP. The optimizer then \emph{reproduces nutrition practice}: nothing in the
water, maximal intake on the bike ($\approx 90$ g/h), reduced intake on the
run. The absorption duals price ``gut training'' directly: at 70.3, one extra
kJ/min of bike absorption capacity is worth $\approx 1.7$ minutes of race time
--- while at Olympic distance the same relaxation is worth almost nothing (speed
caps bind, not energy). Electrolyte and hydration intake, though practically
crucial, are deliberately not modeled: no defensible linear mechanism links
them to speed, and the data cannot calibrate one --- a scope boundary, not an
oversight.

pacing; the athlete ran 115.2 with a dramatic positive split: km 1--10 at

\section{Integrating the Pipeline}
\label{sec:coherence}

Presenting three models in sequence raises two fair objections: that the
models are only loosely coupled, and that the order of solution was chosen
rather than justified. Both are addressed here.

\subsection{Are the objectives coherent?}

Model~1 maximizes a Banister performance index
$p_T = k_1 \text{CTL}_T - k_2 \text{ATL}_T$, whereas the quantity the athlete
actually cares about is the finishing time Model~3 computes. These need not
agree --- and by default they do not. Model~3's energy budget
$E = k_E \text{CTL}_T$ does not involve $\text{ATL}$ at all, so as far as
Model~3 is concerned the ideal preparation is maximal fitness at \emph{any}
level of fatigue, i.e.\ no taper. The disagreement is easy to miss while the
models are solved separately, but it is stark once they are composed: pushing
four materially different Model~1 plans through Model~3 returns \emph{exactly
the same} race time of 140.18 min for all four (Table~\ref{tab:coherence}).
With a fitness-only budget the pacing model is simply blind to the training
decision, and the ``pipeline'' does not really transmit anything.

Making the budget depend on race-day \emph{form} repairs this. Let
\[
E(\text{plan}) \;=\; E_{\text{base}} + k_E\bigl(\text{CTL}_T - \lambda\,\text{ATL}_T\bigr).
\]
If $\lambda = k_2/k_1$ then $E = E_{\text{base}} + (k_E/k_1)\, p_T$, an
increasing affine function of Model~1's objective. Since Model~3's optimal time
is non-increasing in $E$ (verified as a monotonicity test), it follows that
\[
\arg\max p_T \;=\; \arg\max E \;=\; \arg\min \text{race time}.
\]
Model~1's objective is then not a proxy but a \emph{derived} one, and the ratio
$k_2/k_1$ ceases to be a free parameter: it is the fatigue sensitivity of the
energy budget. Empirically the two rankings coincide exactly under
$\lambda = k_2/k_1$ and fail to coincide under $\lambda = 0$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:coherence}).

$\lambda < \text{CTL}_T/\text{ATL}_T$, which at the optimum is

\emph{cannot} be made coherent --- it implies a negative energy budget. This is
exactly where the objective-coefficient ranging of
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} becomes load-bearing: the optimal plan is
invariant for $k_2 \in [1.29, 2.95]$, so any $\lambda \in [1.29, 1.75)$ is
simultaneously coherent, feasible, and plan-preserving. Setting
$\lambda = k_2 = 1.5$ makes the whole system consistent while changing not a
single one of the 144 optimal hour variables. The sensitivity analysis is not
decorative here; it is what licenses the repair.

\subsection{Resolving the circular dependency}

The second objection is that the solution order was imposed. Model~1 needs a
goal race --- the A-race's distance class fixes the macrocycle length and
volume ceiling --- while Model~2 needs a fitness trajectory, since its readiness
gate is driven by the sustained load Model~1 produces. Each is an input to the
other.

Rather than assert an order, we iterate to a fixed point: solve Model~1 under
the macrocycle implied by the current A-race class, pass its average build-phase
load $\bar L$ into Model~2's readiness projection, re-solve Model~2, and take
the distance class of the selected A-race as the next macrocycle. The iteration
converged in two steps (Table~\ref{tab:loop}), and it immediately exposed an
inconsistency in the sequential presentation: the optimal calendar selects a
70.3 as the season's A-race, which calls for a 20-week build, whereas the
original analysis assumed a 16-week Olympic macrocycle. The self-consistent
plan is a 20-week 70.3 macrocycle aimed at Costa Navarino --- which is, fittingly,
the race the athlete actually contested in 2025.

\section{What the Models Recommend}
\label{sec:recommendations}

The models were built to be solved, but their purpose is to change decisions.
Each item below is traceable to a specific result rather than to coaching
opinion.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Train more, and much easier.} Realised training over the last 16
weeks was 6.6\,h/week with $37\%$ of time at moderate-or-hard intensity; the
optimum is 9.0\,h/week with $15\%$ --- less volume at double the intensity
share, the classic anti-polarised pattern. The heuristic benchmark shows the
opposite error is equally costly: filling the hours at high intensity breaches
the fatigue ceiling in 13 of 16 weeks. The recommendation is more easy volume,
not more training.

\item \textbf{Recovery capacity, not free time, is binding.} RHS ranging shows
the objective saturating at 10.5\,h/week, beyond which extra hours have zero
shadow price because the ATL ceiling binds first. Buying training time is
worthless; raising tolerable fatigue (sleep, nutrition, load management) is not,
and the ATL duals peak in late build, which is when that investment pays.

\item \textbf{Build endurance, not peak power.} Below the current 260\,W each
watt is worth $\approx 6$\,s of Olympic-distance time, but the marginal value
collapses to nearly zero at 260--270\,W --- with a fixed energy budget, extra
power cannot be fuelled. One CTL point is worth 69--84\,s up to
$\text{CTL} \approx 85$.

\item \textbf{Ride steady, run hard, pace the run evenly.} The optimum rides
the whole bike leg at Z3 and splits the run between Z3 and the Z5 cap. The 70.3
validation prices the alternative: going out at $4{:}53$/km and fading to
$5{:}52$/km with heart rate dropping from 162 to 145\,bpm cost roughly 29
minutes against the even-paced schedule.

\item \textbf{Fuel at the guideline rate; train the gut only for long course.}
The calibrated intake of $\approx 21$\,kJ/min recovers the standard 75\,g
carbohydrate/hour. Absorption duals price gut training at $\approx 1.7$ min per
extra kJ/min of bike capacity at 70.3, and at almost nothing at Olympic
distance.

\item \textbf{Protect the calendar, not the budget.} Six of eight selected
races are domestic, and cost ranging shows several stay in even if their value
falls to zero. The scarce resource is neither money (0.094 per euro) nor time
off ($\approx 0$ per day) but calendar space: the Hellenic Championship /
Costa Navarino recovery conflict prices at 56.4, the largest dual in the model.

\item \textbf{Two concrete calendar calls.} The early-season Dubai 70.3 is
correctly excluded --- projected CTL cannot reach the 70.3 readiness threshold
by March. And with Costa Navarino as the A-race the macrocycle should be the
20-week 70.3 build, not the 16-week Olympic one, exactly as the fixed-point
iteration of Section~\ref{sec:coherence} detected.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Three limitations deserve honesty. First, the weekly Banister recursion is a
coarse time step; daily dynamics would shift constants, not the structure of the
optimum. Second, the pipeline is solved by
decomposition rather than as one joint program: Section~\ref{sec:coherence}
replaces the fixed solution order with a fixed-point iteration and shows the
objectives can be made coherent, but a single monolithic model co-optimizing
macrocycle and calendar would still be a substantially larger MIP --- a natural
extension. Third, several Model~2/3 parameters
(race values, zone speeds, the energy--CTL constant $k_E$) are constructed from
documented assumptions rather than measured; the sensitivity analyses show which
conclusions survive parameter perturbation. The relay variant mentioned in the
proposal was scoped out in favor of depth (duality, network flows) over breadth.

Against these caveats stand results with practical content: periodization and
``steady bike, hard run'' emerge as optimal rather than assumed; shadow prices
translate into athlete-meaningful quantities (seconds per watt, per kJ, per
vacation day); and the analysis exposes which coaching rules are near-optimal (the
80/20 intensity split) and which are not (filling all available hours).

\section{Conclusions}

The project delivers three coupled optimization models solved on real personal
training data, together spanning the course syllabus: LP modeling and Simplex
solving (Models~1, 3), duality and sensitivity analysis (shadow prices, RHS
ranging, parametric sweeps), integer programming with Branch \& Bound and
integrality-gap analysis (Model~2), and network flows with total unimodularity
(the longest-path reduction). The optimizer independently reproduces core
endurance-coaching principles and, through its dual variables, prices the
athlete's true bottlenecks: recovery capacity in training, money in race
selection, and aerobic endurance --- not peak power --- on race day. All code, data
assumptions and results are reproducible with a single command from the public
repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/manos1503/triathlon-optimization}}

\section{Appendix: the Simplex Method on a Reduced Instance}
\label{sec:simplex}

CBC solves every LP in this project with the (dual) Simplex algorithm. To make
the method visible rather than implicit, Model~3 is reduced to two variables:
the run leg with two zones,
\[
\min\; t_3 + t_5
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
0.2317\, t_3 + 0.2704\, t_5 \ge 10 \;\;(\text{distance}), \qquad
71.3\, t_3 + 95.1\, t_5 \le 3300 \;\;(\text{energy}), \qquad t \ge 0,
\]
with speeds (km/min) and energy rates (kJ/min) taken from the athlete's Z3/Z5
run parameters and a 3300\,kJ energy share for the leg.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.48\textwidth]{simplex_toy.png}
\caption{Feasible region and vertices of the reduced pacing LP. The optimum is
vertex $B$, the intersection of the two constraints.}
\label{fig:toy}
\end{figure}

The feasible region (Fig.~\ref{fig:toy}) has vertices $A = (43.16, 0)$
(all-Z3, time 43.16, energy slack 223\,kJ), $B = (21.30, 18.73)$ (both
constraints binding, time 40.03) and the infeasible all-Z5 corner
$C = (0, 34.70)$. In standard form the problem gains a surplus variable $s_1$
(distance), a slack $s_2$ (energy) and one artificial variable for Phase~I.
Phase~I drives the artificial variable out, landing at vertex $A$ with basis
$\{t_3, s_2\}$; in Phase~II the reduced cost of $t_5$ is negative, so $t_5$
enters and $s_2$ leaves --- the ratio test blocks at the energy constraint ---
and the basis $\{t_3, t_5\}$ at $B$ is optimal, both reduced costs being
nonnegative. The path $A \to B$ is exactly one Simplex pivot;
CBC reports the same optimum, $t = 40.03$ minutes. The duals at $B$ solve
$m^{\top} y = c$ with $m$ the binding-constraint matrix: distance is priced at
$8.64$ min/km and energy at $-0.014$ min/kJ. Both are larger in magnitude than
the full model's run-distance and energy duals ($6.32$ and $-0.0071$), as they
should be: the toy has no other zones or legs to absorb a perturbation, so each
marginal km or kJ hits harder.
